Oddech

The air in your room has a shape. Oddech, Polish for breath, is a small robot that finds it. It maps the fine dust in a room, works out what reaches the people in it, says so in plain words, and tells the window and the purifier what to do.

01
The problem

Kraków breathes better than ten years ago. Not well enough.

The city banned solid fuel at home and the air improved. It is still above the limit, the smoke still drifts in from the towns around it, and in the cold months it comes indoors with us.

Why here

A valley that holds its smoke

Kraków is a pollution hot spot in Europe. It lies in a concave landform. In the heating season smog episodes follow from poor natural ventilation and frequent temperature inversions. Solid-fuel heating in the towns around the city is the main source of the smoke that reaches it, and the city's position makes it collect pollution from its neighbours.

Our diagnosis

Ecological or health problem? Both, at opposite ends. At the source it is an energy and heating problem, fixed by policy over years. At the receiving end it is a health problem, decided indoors, room by room and hour by hour. Oddech works at the receiving end, where a person can act today.

02
The diagnosis

Twelve hard questions, before anything is built.

The questions a robotics and computer-science examiner would ask, answered with evidence, a design choice, or an honest “to be tested”. Each answer is tagged.

EvidenceDesign choiceTo be testedKill criterion
01Is indoor air in Kraków really the problem?Evidence

Yes. In the heating season indoor air follows the outdoor smog, and rooms add their own.

PM2.5 made up about 70 % of indoor PM10 in Kraków flats in the cold half-year. In naturally ventilated Silesian kindergartens indoor PM2.5 did not differ significantly from outdoor. In Barcelona schools PM2.5 was 1.7 times the urban background, largely from sources inside the school.

02Why not just buy an air purifier?EvidenceDesign

Buy one. Oddech does not replace it; it tells it when, where and how hard to work, and tells people why.

Purifiers work: in a randomized crossover trial in 17 elementary schools, classrooms with HEPA cleaners had 39.9 % lower average PM2.5. But a purifier cleans the air near it and reads one point. Indoor air is not well mixed: in a tracer-gas study in two homes, concentrations within 0.25 m of an active source averaged 6 to 20 times the well-mixed prediction. The well-mixed assumption itself “needs to be revisited”. A map says where the purifier should stand, and when opening a window helps more than filtering.

03Why a robot, and not ten fixed sensors?To be testedKill criterion

One good sensor that moves solves the placement problem again every few minutes; ten cheap ones each need calibrating.

Low-cost units vary from unit to unit. Choosing where to place sensors is itself hard: maximising the information they give is NP-complete. In our simulation of 24 rooms, three fixed sensors plus Oddech beat four fixed sensors where people breathe in every room (H1, section 06).

Kill criterion. If the rooms we target turn out to be well mixed within the sensor's own uncertainty, the robot is not needed: we drop it and ship the software with one sensor.

04The dust is airborne. Why a ground robot and not a drone?Design choice

Because a rotor stirs the very air it is trying to measure.

Aerial robots are used for gas mapping. Indoors near children, our engineering judgement goes the other way: downwash mixes the field, rotors are loud, flight lasts minutes rather than hours, and a falling drone is a hazard. Oddech drives, stops, and only then raises a telescoping mast, sampling at 0.40, 1.10 and 1.60 m: a child on the floor, a seated adult, a standing adult.

05How does it decide where to go next?DesignTo be tested

It goes where its map is least sure about the air that people actually breathe, if the trip is worth it.

The map is probabilistic: a mean and an uncertainty everywhere. The textbook choice is the place of highest variance; mutual information does better. Oddech weights uncertainty by where people sit and divides by travel time. Physics enters as the prior, the room's mass balance ; in WP1 a physics-informed neural network replaces the browser's estimator.

06Is mass in µg/m³ even the right number? What about toxicity, mitochondria?EvidenceDesign

Not entirely. Mass is what can be sensed in a home; toxicity has to be sampled and analysed.

Across Europe, oxidative potential, a measure of how strongly particles drive oxidative stress, comes mostly from anthropogenic sources, residential biomass burning among them, and cutting mass alone may not cut it. Biomass-related PM2.5 caused mitochondrial dysfunction in human airway cells: lower membrane potential, more mitochondrial ROS, less ATP. Online oxidative-potential instruments exist, but at laboratory scale. So: mass on board, toxicity off board. Oddech can carry a filter sampler to breathing height for the lab.

07Can a cheap optical sensor be trusted?EvidenceDesign

Only with calibration, a humidity correction, and honest ranges.

Readings rise sharply above about 75 % relative humidity, because particles take up water. A κ-Köhler correction brought one optical counter to within 33 % of a reference instrument. Oddech carries a humidity sensor, applies the correction, is co-located with a reference before each deployment, and always speaks in ranges.

08Will people act on it, and trust it the right amount?To be tested

Unknown. That is the third experiment.

People fail to rely on automation appropriately; trust should match reliability. Humanness cues change how a chatbot is perceived. WP3 crosses persona (neutral or human-like) with uncertainty (hidden or stated): four versions of the same advice, which you can switch between in the simulation.

09Who pays?Hypothesis

First, institutions where one room serves many people and staff decide for them.

Kindergartens, schools and care homes: a yearly service including the robot, calibration and reports. Families through software on the monitors and purifiers they already own. City clean-air programmes as co-funders of pilots. All of it is a hypothesis we test in interviews (section 09).

10Is it safe and private in a home with children?Design choice

No camera, speech on the device, mast down while moving, slow near people.

Navigation uses a laser scanner, so the robot never films anyone. The language model runs on board; health talk stays at home, under EU health-data rules. Disclosing emotions to a chatbot had the same effects as disclosing them to a person , which is exactly why the data must not leave. The mast is raised only when the robot stands still; speed halves within about a metre of a person.

11Isn't this just Ecovacs' AIRBOT?Evidence

No. AIRBOT moves a filter. Oddech moves a measurement, and can steer any purifier, AIRBOT included.

AIRBOT Z1 is a mobile purifier with a 600 m³/h clean-air delivery rate that senses PM2.5, temperature and humidity on board and cleans an area when a linked monitor crosses a threshold. It does not build a room map with uncertainty, read the air at several heights, estimate a dose or explain it. Those are the parts Oddech adds.

12What would prove us wrong?Kill criteria

We state them now, so they cannot move later.

  • H1 fails in the chamber: a moving sensor does not beat fixed ones at equal cost.
  • Target rooms are well mixed within sensor uncertainty (see 03): drop the robot, keep the software.
  • Wording makes no difference to what people do, whatever we try (H3a): the interface is not our edge.
  • No institution will pay for a pilot after the interviews: switch to licensing the software to purifier makers.

The dose
Dr(T) = ∫T0 C(t) · V̇E · DFr(dp)Ar dt [µg cm⁻²]

C is the concentration where the person breathes (the map), V̇E the breathing rate, DFr the deposition fraction in airway region r for particle size dp, as in the ICRP lung model , and Ar the region's surface area.

The bridge

Micrograms of PM2.5 per square centimetre of airway is both what deposition physics computes and the dose that airway-cell studies use.

Bronchial epithelial cells were dosed at 1 and 30 µg/cm² ; cells from people with asthma and especially COPD responded more strongly than healthy ones. So the robot can speak about the body, not only the air.

04
The robot

Small enough for a kindergarten. Tall enough for a standing adult.

The size of a robot vacuum, with a telescoping mast that lifts the sensor to where people breathe. These are design targets, not a built prototype.

1
Sensor head

Optical particle counter (PM1, PM2.5, PM10 and size bins), humidity and temperature, CO₂ and VOC. Air enters at the top and leaves downward, away from the robot's own heat.

2
Telescoping mast

Four nested 32 cm stages on a lead screw stow inside the body and lift the inlet from 0.40 to 1.60 m. It rises only when the robot stands still.

3
Light ring and voice

Indigo for clean air, terracotta for heavy air; microphones and a speaker for plain-language advice.

4
Laser scanner band

2D LiDAR for mapping and navigation, cliff sensors and a bumper. No camera, by design.

5
On-board computer

Map, planner and a small language model run locally; nothing about the household leaves the device.

6
Battery and dock

14.4 V lithium-ion, about 100 Wh. Returns to its dock below 20 %.

7
Drive

Two hub motors and a caster: at most 0.30 m/s, and 0.15 m/s within about a metre of a person.

8
Filter sampler, optional

A 37 mm filter cassette and a small pump collect particles at breathing height for the lab: oxidative potential, airway-cell assays.

05
The sensor

How a low-cost sensor sees a particle, and how it is fooled.

An optical particle counter does not weigh dust. It counts flashes of scattered laser light and converts them into mass with assumptions.

  1. A fan draws air through a dark chamber.
  2. A laser beam crosses the air stream.
  3. Each particle scatters a flash onto a photodiode; a bigger particle gives a bigger pulse.
  4. Pulses are sorted by size into bins and counted.
  5. Counts become mass with an assumed shape and density. Water on the particles is counted as dust.
Humidity, the known trap

Mass growth factor f = 1 + (κ ρw/ρp) · aw/(1 − aw), with aw = RH and ρp = 1.65 g/cm³; the κ form follows as applied to an optical counter by . Readings rise above about 75 % RH.

Mass on board, toxicity off board

What Oddech measures, and where

On board · every second

Particles, humidity, CO₂, VOC

PM mass and size from the optical counter; humidity for the correction; CO₂ says whether the room is ventilated; VOC flags cooking and cleaning sprays.

On board · collected for the lab

Filter samples at breathing height

Oxidative potential by the DTT assay, and airway-cell responses including mitochondrial endpoints: membrane potential, mitochondrial ROS, ATP. This is Thea's dose–response work.

Not on a home robot yet

Toxicity in real time

Online oxidative-potential instruments exist but are laboratory-scale, with a mist chamber and syringe pumps. Living-cell sensors need culture conditions. We watch this space; we do not promise it.

Mass is not toxicity. Across Europe, cutting particle mass alone may not cut its oxidative potential, which comes mostly from sources such as residential biomass burning.

The air

A three-dimensional model of an 8 × 6 × 2.7 m flat on a 20 cm grid: fine dust is carried by the air, spread by turbulence, deposited on surfaces, exchanged with the outside, and removed by the purifier. The flows from the purifier, the cooking plume and the open window are built to conserve mass exactly. This is the “truth” the robot never sees directly.

The map

Oddech sees only noisy readings: its own mast at three heights and the household monitor on the bookshelf. A Gaussian process on log concentration, with the room's mass balance as its prior, turns them into a map with an uncertainty everywhere. In WP1 a physics-informed neural network takes this role.

The robot

It picks its next stop by the policy you choose, plans a path around the furniture with A*, keeps clear of people, drives, stops, raises the mast, reads, lowers it, and returns to its dock when the battery runs low. Its advice comes in the four wordings of the WP3 experiment.

Why now

The cold half-year is the smog season, and indoor air follows it.

A child on the floor breathes at about 0.4 m, below the shelf where a monitor tends to sit. Diseased airway cells respond more strongly to the same dose. And the EU limit for PM2.5 falls from 25 to 10 µg/m³ in 2030. Institutions will be asked what they did about the air inside.

Research questions

Four questions, each with a way to fail.

Hypotheses, measures, baselines and pass marks are set before the data, not after.

RQ1 · sensing · Julian

Does a moving sensor map the air people breathe better than fixed ones, at equal cost?

H1
Three fixed sensors and one uncertainty-guided robot beat four fixed sensors.
Measure
Error of log PM2.5 where people breathe and across the floor; coverage of the 90 % intervals.
Baselines
Four fixed; three fixed and a robot sent to random places.
Status
Supported in our simulation (section 06). Untested in a chamber or a real room.
RQ2 · body · Thea

Where does a day indoors in Kraków sit on the airway-cell dose axis, and what responds there?

Approach
Dose table (µg/cm², adult and child, by airway region) against published dose–response data.
Endpoints
Gene expression pathways ; mitochondrial function ; healthy against diseased models.
Status
Descriptive; no direction assumed.
RQ3 · words · Aleksandra

Does a human-like voice make people act, and does stating uncertainty keep trust in line with accuracy?

Design
2 × 2 between subjects: persona × uncertainty; a second scenario where the robot was wrong.
Sample
128 people (32 per cell) for a medium effect, f = 0.25, α = 0.05, power 0.8.
Status
The pilot with programme participants tests the materials, not the hypotheses.
RQ4 · decisions · all of us

Does advice about dose and body lead to better decisions than the bare number?

Measure
Actions taken and exposure avoided in the simulated room, then in a pilot room.
Baseline
The same room with a monitor's number only.
Status
After the programme.
Validation ladder
  1. SimulationKnown truth; three sensing strategies; this site.
  2. ChamberA sealed room, a controlled source, a reference instrument.
  3. One real roomA kindergarten or care-home room, with consent.
  4. PilotSeveral rooms through a smog season.
Threats to validity

Simulation to reality. Real flows, people and furniture move; the chamber step exists to catch this.

Sensors. Unit bias, drift and humidity ; co-location with a reference instrument and a stated range on every reading.

People. Novelty, being watched, and a small, educated pilot sample; the pilot tests materials only.

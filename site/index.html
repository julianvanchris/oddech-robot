Oddech

Every air monitor weighs the dust. Oddech asks living cells how much it hurts. Oddech, Polish for breath, is a small robot that carries real airway cells to where people breathe. It maps the dust in a room, lets the cells tell how harmful that air is, says so in plain words, and tells the window, the purifier and your phone what to do.

01
The problem

Kraków breathes better than ten years ago. Not well enough.

The city banned solid fuel at home and the air improved. It is still above the limit, the smoke still drifts in from the towns around it, and in the cold months it comes indoors with us.

Why here

A valley that holds its smoke

Kraków is a pollution hot spot in Europe. It lies in a concave landform. In the heating season smog episodes follow from poor natural ventilation and frequent temperature inversions. Solid-fuel heating in the towns around the city is the main source of the smoke that reaches it, and the city's position makes it collect pollution from its neighbours.

Our diagnosis

Ecological or health problem? Both, at opposite ends. At the source it is an energy and heating problem, fixed by policy over years. At the receiving end it is a health problem, decided indoors, room by room and hour by hour. Oddech works at the receiving end, where a person can act today.

Questions people ask us

Twelve honest questions, in plain words.

What a parent, a kindergarten director or an investor would ask before trusting a robot with the air. Each answer says whether it rests on evidence, on a design choice, or still has to be tested.

EvidenceDesign choiceTo be testedWould stop us
01Is the air inside Kraków homes really a problem?Evidence

Yes. In winter the smog comes indoors, and rooms add dust of their own.

In Kraków flats in the cold half-year, about 70 % of indoor PM10 was the finest dust, PM2.5. In Silesian kindergartens, indoor PM2.5 did not differ significantly from outdoor. In Barcelona schools PM2.5 was 1.7 times the urban background, much of it from inside the school.

02Why not just buy an air purifier?EvidenceDesign

Keep it. Oddech makes it smarter: where to stand, when to run, and when an open window helps more.

Purifiers work: in a trial in 17 elementary schools, classrooms with HEPA cleaners had 39.9 % lower PM2.5. But a purifier cleans the air around itself and shows one number. Next to a source, the air can be 6 to 20 times the well-mixed prediction (a tracer-gas study in two homes). The idea that a room is evenly mixed “needs to be revisited”.

03Why a moving robot, and not many fixed sensors?To be testedWould stop us

One good sensor that moves can do the work of many cheap ones, which each need checking.

Cheap sensors vary from unit to unit. Choosing the best spots for fixed sensors is itself a famously hard problem. And living cells cannot be put in every corner: one cartridge has to travel. In our simulation of 24 rooms, three sensors plus Oddech read the air where people breathe better than four sensors, in every room (section 06).

What would stop us. If the rooms we serve turn out evenly mixed, the wheels are not needed: we keep the living sensor and the app, in a fixed station.

04Dust floats in the air. Why a robot on wheels and not a drone?Design choice

Because propellers stir the very air they are trying to measure.

Flying robots are used to map gases. Indoors, next to children, they are loud, fly for minutes rather than hours, and can fall; and they cannot keep cells warm. Oddech drives, stops, and raises its mast to the height of a child on the floor, a seated adult and a standing adult.

05How does it know where to go?DesignTo be tested

It goes where it is least sure about the air people actually breathe, if the trip is worth it.

Its map shows both a best guess and how sure it is. The room's physics, how dust enters, spreads and settles, gives the map a sensible starting point ; each reading then sharpens it. In the next step, a physics-guided AI takes over the map.

06Is the amount of dust the whole story? What about the harm to our bodies?EvidenceDesign

No. Two dusts of the same weight can harm very differently. So Oddech asks living cells.

Across Europe, the oxidising power of dust comes mostly from human sources such as residential biomass burning, and cutting mass alone may not cut it. Smoke from biomass burning damaged the mitochondria, the power plants of human airway cells: lower membrane potential, more mitochondrial ROS, less ATP. Oddech's Living Air Sensor uses exactly that response: airway cells whose mitochondrial dye glows when the air stresses them (section 05).

07Can a cheap dust sensor be trusted?EvidenceDesign

Only if it is checked, corrected for humidity, and honest about its range.

In damp air particles take up water and look heavier: readings rise above about 75 % humidity. A κ-Köhler humidity correction brought one counter to within 33 % of a reference instrument. Oddech corrects, is checked against a reference before each deployment, and always speaks in ranges.

08Will people listen, and trust it the right amount?To be tested

We do not assume it. We test it.

Trusting a machine too much is as risky as not trusting it at all: people fail to rely on automation appropriately. How human a chatbot seems changes how people react. We test four wordings of the same advice, which you can switch between in the simulation.

09Who pays?Hypothesis

First, places where one room serves many people and staff decide for them.

Kindergartens, schools and care homes: a monthly service with the robot, living-sensor cartridges and the cloud dashboard. Families through the app. City clean-air programmes as co-funders. Section 10 shows the numbers and the plan B for each.

10Is it safe and private in a home with children?Design choice

No camera. Conversations stay on the robot. Only air readings go to the cloud, encrypted, with consent.

It finds its way with a laser scanner, so it never films anyone. What people say to it is handled on the robot: disclosing emotions to a chatbot had the same effects as disclosing them to a person , which is why it must stay private. The cells sit sealed in a cartridge, and the robot slows down near people.

11Isn't this an existing idea? Isn't it just the Ecovacs AIRBOT?Evidence

The pieces exist. The combination does not, as far as our search found. Section 03·b lays it out.

AIRBOT Z1 is a mobile purifier with a 600 m³/h clean-air delivery rate that senses PM2.5, temperature and humidity and cleans an area when a linked monitor crosses a threshold. It moves a filter. Oddech carries living cells to where people breathe, maps harm rather than dust, and can steer any purifier, AIRBOT included.

12What would prove us wrong?Would stop us

We say it now, so it cannot move later.

  • In a test chamber, a moving sensor does no better than fixed ones.
  • Real rooms are evenly mixed (question 03): we drop the wheels.
  • The cells' glow does not track exposure in the lab: we fall back to filter samples tested in a lab.
  • No institution will pay for a pilot: we license the software to purifier makers.

The dose
Dr(T) = ∫T0 C(t) · V̇E · DFr(dp)Ar dt [µg cm⁻²]

C is the concentration where the person breathes (the map), V̇E the breathing rate, DFr the deposition fraction in airway region r for particle size dp, as in the ICRP lung model , and Ar the region's surface area.

The bridge

Micrograms of PM2.5 per square centimetre of airway is what deposition physics computes, and the dose airway-cell studies use. The Living Air Sensor turns it into a signal.

Bronchial epithelial cells were dosed at 1 and 30 µg/cm² ; cells from people with asthma and especially COPD responded more strongly than healthy ones. So the robot can speak about the body, not only the air.

04
The robot

Small enough for a kindergarten. Tall enough for a standing adult.

The size of a robot vacuum, with a telescoping mast that lifts its nose to where people breathe, and a bay of living cells inside. Design targets, not a built prototype.

1
Nose: the sensor head

Breathes air in at the top. A dust counter (PM1, PM2.5, PM10), humidity, temperature, CO₂ and VOC sensors.

2
Telescoping mast

Four nested 32 cm stages stow inside the body and lift the nose from 0.40 to 1.60 m. It rises only when the robot stands still.

3
Living Air Sensor bay

An 8-well cartridge of airway cells kept near body temperature in a dark chamber, read by light (section 05). Swapped like a capsule.

4
Lungs: filter and exhaust

Everything it breathes in leaves through a HEPA and carbon filter at the base: it exhales cleaner air than it took in.

5
Light ring and voice

Indigo for clean air, terracotta for heavy air; microphones and a speaker for plain-language advice.

6
Laser scanner band

Finds its way and avoids people with a laser scanner, cliff sensors and a bumper. No camera, by design.

7
Computer and cloud link

The map, the planner and a small language model run on board; air readings sync to Oddech Cloud (section 07).

8
Battery, drive and dock

14.4 V lithium-ion, about 100 Wh; two hub motors, at most 0.30 m/s; back to the dock below 20 %.

      Temperature and humidity
      Body-warm and airway-moist, or the reading does not count
      
      
      Room air in a Kraków winter is cool and dry; airway cells live at body temperature in moist air. Get this wrong and the cells would report the climate, not the dust.
      
    
      
      
      37 °C
      A warm bay
      A heater and a fan hold the cartridge bay at body temperature, for about 6 W of the battery.
      
      
      85–90 % RH
      Moist sample air
      The breath is warmed and moistened before it reaches the cells. A mobile air-liquid interface system, built to enable field studies, holds its cells at 37 °C, 85–90 % RH. 
      
      
      dry air
      Why it matters
      In a cell study, dry air alone reduced airway-cell function and raised inflammation.  A transportable exposure chamber controls temperature and humidity by computer, preventing cellular stress. 
      
      
      in range
      Every cycle is checked
      Temperature and humidity are logged in the bay; a cycle counts only if both stayed in range. The lines are heated so nothing condenses on the wells, and the controls share every condition.
      
    
      

    
      What the cells are
      A thin airway lining, grown on a membrane
      
      
      Bronchial cells grown at the air-liquid interface form a lining of ciliated, goblet, club and basal cells, fed from below and open to the air above.  A dye makes their mitochondria glow when the air stresses them: filtered air leaves a faint glow, smoky room air a bright one.
      
    
      

    
      How fast, and at what price
      Read in minutes, for cents a reading
      
      
      A dust counter answers every second but only weighs the dust. A classic test of harm takes a day or more and uses the cells up. Living cells sit in between: fast enough to act on, cheap enough to read all day. Our design reads them every ten minutes; whether a cartridge in a robot keeps that pace is the first lab test.
      
    
      
    
      
      
      within minutes
      Cells answer fast
      In a lab, airway cells reacted to nanoparticles within minutes of application, far faster than the toxicity tests usually reported.  Airway cells exposed to ozone have been watched in real time. 
      
      
      real time
      Read alive, not used up
      Live-cell sensors read oxidative stress with a time resolution conventional methods cannot reach, and conventional methods destroy the sample. 
      
      
      hourly · 24 h
      What else there is
      The best automated chemical test of harm gives hourly readings from a lab-sized set-up ; a classic cell test exposes cells for 24 h before the analysis. 
      
      
      cents
      A reading costs cents
      One cartridge gives about 1,000 readings a week. Planning assumptions, not quotes.

      Why eight wells
      Four exposed replicates, two filtered controls, one optical reference, one blank: the logic of a good lab experiment, inside a cartridge. The format is two 4-well glass chamber slides, 1.7 cm² per well. 
      
      
      Keeping cells alive
      Airway cells grown at an air-liquid interface meet the air the way our airways do.  The bay keeps them near 37 °C (about 6 W); a cartridge is swapped weekly.
      
      
      What it adds
      Smoke-like dust raises the glow more than cooking dust of the same mass. That is the gap between weighing the air and knowing its harm. 
      
      
      What is not yet proven
      Thea's concept is a design, not a measurement. Whether the glow tracks exposure reliably, and how long cells stay stable in a robot, is the first thing to test in the lab (section 11).

      How a low-cost sensor sees a particle, and how it is fooled.
      
      
      An optical particle counter does not weigh dust. It counts flashes of scattered laser light and turns them into mass with assumptions.

        2. 
        
      2. A laser beam crosses the air stream.
        3. 
        
      3. Each particle scatters a flash onto a photodiode; a bigger particle gives a bigger pulse.
        4. 
        
      4. Pulses are sorted by size into bins and counted.
        5. 
        
      5. Counts become mass with an assumed shape and density. Water on the particles is counted as dust.

      The air
      A three-dimensional model of an 8 × 6 × 2.7 m flat on a 20 cm grid: fine dust is carried by the air, spread by turbulence, deposited on surfaces, exchanged with the outside, and removed by the purifier. A second field tracks how harmful the dust is, by its source. This is the “truth” the robot never sees directly.
      
      
      The map
      Oddech sees only noisy readings: its own mast at three heights and the household monitor. A statistical map on log concentration, starting from the room's mass balance, turns them into a best guess and a doubt everywhere. Next, a physics-guided AI takes this role. 
      
      
      The robot
      It picks its next stop, plans a path around the furniture, keeps clear of people, drives, stops, raises the mast, reads, lowers it, runs a living-sensor cycle every ten minutes, and docks when its battery runs low. Its advice comes in four wordings.

      07
      
      
      Oddech Cloud · the IoT layer
      
      
      Every room, live, on one screen.
      
      
      The robot sends what it measured, never what it heard or saw. Staff see every room in real time, parents get an alert on the phone, and the city sees anonymous trends.
      
    
      
    
      
    
      
      
      01 · robot
      Thinks at the edge
      Map, planner, voice and the living-sensor maths run on board. Every ten seconds it sends a small summary: air where people breathe, map doubt, stress index, purifier and battery.
      
      
      02 · link
      Encrypted, light
      A few hundred bytes per message over an encrypted IoT protocol (MQTT over TLS). If the Wi-Fi drops, the robot keeps working and syncs later.
      
      
      03 · cloud
      Stores, alerts, learns
      A time-series store keeps the history; rules raise alerts (air above the WHO 24-hour guideline, stress rising, cartridge due); models improve across rooms.
      
      
      04 · people
      Sees, acts, reports
      A live dashboard for staff, a phone app for parents, a monthly report for the director, and consented, anonymous statistics for the city.

          Why now
          The cold half-year is the smog season, and indoor air follows it. 
          
      
          A child on the floor breathes at about 0.4 m, below the shelf where a monitor tends to sit. Diseased airway cells respond more strongly to the same dose.  And the EU limit for PM2.5 falls from 25 to 10 µg/m³ in 2030.  Institutions will be asked what they did about the air inside.

          11
          
      
          Research questions
          
      
          Four questions, each with a way to fail.
          
      
          Hypotheses, measures, baselines and pass marks are set before the data, not after.
          
    
          
    
          
      
          RQ1 · sensing · Julian · Antonio
          Does a moving sensor map the air people breathe better than fixed ones, at equal cost?
          
        
          H1
          Three fixed sensors and one uncertainty-guided robot beat four fixed sensors.
          Measure
          Error of log PM2.5 where people breathe and across the floor.
          Baselines
          Four fixed; three fixed and a robot sent to random places.
          Status
          Supported in our simulation (section 06). Untested in a chamber or a real room.
          
      
          RQ2 · living sensor · Thea
          Does the cells' mitochondrial glow track exposure, and add information beyond dust mass?
          
        
          Approach
          Controlled PM2.5 exposures in the lab give a calibration curve; smoke against dust of equal mass tests the added value.
          Endpoints
          Mitochondrial ROS and membrane potential ; air-liquid interface cultures ; healthy against diseased cells. 
          Status
          Concept; no experimental data yet.
          
      
          RQ3 · words · Aleksandra
          Does a human-like voice make people act, and does stating uncertainty keep trust in line with accuracy?
          
        
          Design
          2 × 2 between subjects: persona × uncertainty; a second scenario where the robot was wrong. 
          Sample
          128 people (32 per cell) for a medium effect, f = 0.25, α = 0.05, power 0.8. 
          Status
          The pilot with programme participants tests the materials, not the hypotheses.
          
      
          RQ4 · decisions · all of us
          Does advice about harm and body lead to better decisions than the bare number?
          
        
          Measure
          Actions taken and exposure avoided in the simulated room, then in a pilot room.
          Baseline
          The same room with a monitor's number only.
          Status
          After the programme.
          
    
          
    
          
      
          Validation ladder
          
      
          1. SimulationKnown truth; three sensing strategies; this site.
          2. Lab and chamberLiving-sensor calibration; a sealed room with a reference instrument.
          3. One real roomA kindergarten or care-home room, with consent.
          4. PilotSeveral rooms through a smog season, on Oddech Cloud.
          
    
          
    
          
      
          Threats to validity
          
      
          
        
          Simulation to reality. Real flows, people and furniture move; the chamber step exists to catch this.
          
        
          Sensors and cells. Unit bias, drift and humidity ; for the cells, donor-to-donor variation, which matched controls reduce but do not remove.
          
        
          People. Novelty, being watched, and a small, educated pilot sample; the pilot tests materials only.

          Risks
          What could go wrong, and our answer
          
    
          
      
          Cells drift or tire
          Controls, a lamp reference and a blank in every cartridge; a fresh cartridge each week. Plan B: the robot collects filter samples and a partner lab tests them.
          
      
          Heat, dry air, condensation
          A 37 °C bay, sample air kept moist, heated lines; a cycle counts only if temperature and humidity stayed in range (section 05).
          
      
          Biological waste
          A sealed cartridge with its own filter, returned in its pouch and inactivated by the partner lab as laboratory waste. 
          
      
          Safety around children
          Slow near people, the mast down while it drives, a soft bumper, no camera.
          
      
          Rooms turn out evenly mixed
          Plan B: Oddech Station, a fixed living sensor with the cloud.
          
      
          No one pays
          Plan B: license the robot's brain and cloud to purifier makers.
